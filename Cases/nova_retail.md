## Use case: AI-assisted returns at NovaRetail

NovaRetail is a medium-sized online and physical retailer selling electronics, home products, and clothing.

The company processes approximately **12,000 customer-service requests per month**. Around 40% concern returns, refunds, damaged products, delivery problems, or warranty questions.

Customer-service agents currently need to search across:

* the customer relationship management system;
* order and delivery systems;
* product and warranty databases;
* return policies;
* previous emails;
* spreadsheets maintained by individual departments.

The process is slow and inconsistent:

* average response time: **18 hours**;
* first-contact resolution: **62%**;
* approximately **28% of cases are escalated**;
* refund decisions are sometimes inconsistent;
* agents spend considerable time copying information between systems;
* customers complain that different agents provide different answers.

The Chief Operating Officer proposes:

> “Let us implement an AI customer-service system that automatically answers customers and approves simple refunds.”

The customer-service manager is interested but concerned about incorrect answers, fraudulent refund requests, employee resistance, and loss of customer trust.

The company has a limited budget and wants to run a **six-week pilot** before making a larger investment.

### Available information

NovaRetail has:

* three years of customer-service conversations;
* order, payment, and delivery records;
* written return and warranty policies;
* product information;
* customer satisfaction scores;
* records of refunds and escalations.

However:

* some policies are outdated;
* different departments use different definitions;
* historical responses contain mistakes;
* customer-service data includes personal information;
* refund decisions are not always documented;
* the systems are not fully integrated.

The company is considering several possible interventions:

1. an AI assistant that searches policies and drafts responses for agents;
2. an AI chatbot that answers customers directly;
3. an AI system that recommends whether a refund should be approved;
4. a system that automatically approves low-value refunds;
5. a simpler search and workflow tool without generative AI.

The team must decide whether AI should be used, where it should be used, and under what conditions.

## Student task: complete the AI Implementation Canvas

Your team must design a responsible AI intervention for NovaRetail. You may recommend an AI solution, a simpler technology solution, process redesign, a limited pilot, or no implementation.

Complete the canvas by answering the following questions.

### 1. Strategic objective

What important business or organizational outcome should NovaRetail improve?

Examples include:

* reducing response time;
* improving consistency;
* reducing unnecessary escalations;
* improving customer satisfaction;
* reducing refund losses;
* improving employee productivity.

### 2. Business problem

What is the precise problem?

Who experiences it, and what is the current cost of the problem?

Distinguish between symptoms and root causes.

### 3. Current process

Map the existing returns or customer-service process.

Show:

* customer request;
* information required;
* decisions made;
* systems consulted;
* human roles;
* exceptions and escalations;
* final action.

### 4. Users and stakeholders

Identify the main stakeholders:

* customers;
* customer-service agents;
* customer-service managers;
* finance;
* logistics;
* legal and compliance;
* IT and security;
* senior management.

What does each stakeholder want, and what might each stakeholder fear?

### 5. Role of AI

Define precisely what the AI system would do.

For example, would it:

* retrieve relevant policies;
* summarize a customer case;
* classify the request;
* draft a response;
* recommend an action;
* predict fraud risk;
* approve a refund;
* communicate directly with the customer?

What should the AI explicitly not do?

### 6. Human–AI interaction

Specify:

* what AI does;
* what the employee does;
* when human approval is required;
* when the case must be escalated;
* how employees correct the system;
* who remains accountable for the final decision.

### 7. Data and context

Identify the data required.

For each source, consider:

* accuracy;
* completeness;
* freshness;
* access permissions;
* privacy;
* conflicting information;
* ownership;
* maintenance responsibility.

What information would the AI need in order to provide a reliable answer?

### 8. Workflow redesign

Show the proposed future workflow.

Explain how the process would change for:

* ordinary cases;
* ambiguous cases;
* high-value refunds;
* suspected fraud;
* vulnerable customers;
* policy exceptions;
* system failure.

### 9. Evaluation plan

Define how the pilot would be tested.

Include:

* a baseline;
* representative test cases;
* difficult and unusual cases;
* error categories;
* human evaluation;
* customer impact;
* acceptance criteria.

Possible metrics include:

* response time;
* first-contact resolution;
* escalation rate;
* factual accuracy;
* policy compliance;
* refund error rate;
* customer satisfaction;
* agent correction rate.

### 10. Risks and safeguards

Identify at least five risks.

Consider:

* hallucinated policies;
* incorrect refunds;
* discriminatory treatment;
* privacy violations;
* prompt injection;
* manipulation by customers;
* over-reliance by employees;
* unclear accountability;
* outdated information;
* reputational damage.

For each risk, propose prevention, detection, and recovery measures.

### 11. Business value and economics

Estimate:

* expected benefits;
* implementation costs;
* model and infrastructure costs;
* integration costs;
* employee training costs;
* monitoring and maintenance costs;
* cost of errors;
* expected return on investment.

Compare the proposed AI solution with the no-AI alternative.

### 12. Pilot and implementation plan

Design the smallest credible six-week pilot.

Specify:

* pilot users;
* pilot process;
* excluded cases;
* systems required;
* safeguards;
* training;
* monitoring;
* review points;
* decision at the end of the pilot.

### 13. Final recommendation

Choose one:

* implement a controlled pilot;
* redesign the process before piloting;
* build the data and knowledge foundation first;
* use a simpler non-AI solution;
* collect more evidence;
* postpone;
* do not implement AI.

Your recommendation must be justified by evidence, assumptions, risks, and measurable conditions.

## Final deliverable

Submit:

* the completed AI Implementation Canvas;

* one future-state workflow;
* a pilot evaluation plan;
* a risk-and-safeguard table;
* a final recommendation of approximately 500 words.

The central question is:

> Should NovaRetail allow AI to answer customers and make refund decisions, or should it use AI in a narrower, more controlled role?
